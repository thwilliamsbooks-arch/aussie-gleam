If you own a caravan in Southeast Queensland, you already know the conditions out here don't go easy on your rig. Between the summer humidity, coastal salt air, UV rays that fade everything they touch, and the red dust you pick up heading inland toward the Scenic Rim, keeping your van looking its best takes a bit of know-how. These caravan cleaning tips are designed for SEQ owners who want to protect their investment and keep things road-ready — whether you're a weekend warrior or a grey nomad clocking serious kilometres.

## Why Regular Cleaning Matters More Than You Think

A caravan isn't just something you clean for appearances. Regular cleaning is a core part of caravan maintenance. Dirt, grime, bird droppings, and mould spores — particularly nasty here in the humid subtropics — can degrade sealants, eat into paintwork, and cause long-term damage to the body if left unchecked. The same goes for motorhome cleaning and RV washing: the longer you leave it, the harder (and more expensive) it becomes to fix.

A good rule of thumb for SEQ conditions? Give your caravan a solid exterior wash every four to six weeks, and a thorough interior clean before and after every trip.

## Exterior Cleaning: Where to Start

### Rinse First, Always

Before you scrub anything, give the entire exterior a thorough rinse with a hose. This lifts loose dirt, dust, and debris so you're not grinding it into the surface when you start washing. Work from the roof down — gravity is your friend here.

### Use the Right Products

Avoid household detergents. They can strip wax coatings and dry out rubber seals over time. Instead, use a pH-neutral vehicle shampoo designed for caravans or RVs. If you're doing motorhome cleaning on a fibreglass body, look for products specifically rated for fibreglass — they're gentler and more effective.

A soft-bristled brush on a long handle is ideal for the roof and upper panels. Use a microfibre wash mitt for the sides to avoid scratching the surface.

### Don't Forget the Roof

The roof cops the worst of Queensland's UV and takes the brunt of tree sap, bird droppings, and debris. Get up there safely and check for any lifted sealant around roof vents, skylights, and air conditioners while you're at it. Catching a failing seal early saves you from a nasty water ingress problem down the track.

### Rinse and Dry Properly

After washing, rinse thoroughly — any soap residue left to dry in the Queensland heat will leave streaks and water spots. If possible, dry with a chamois or clean microfibre towels rather than letting it air dry. Pay special attention to corners, joins, and door frames where water loves to pool.

## Interior Cleaning: Tackling Humidity and Mould

Southeast Queensland's humidity is the caravan owner's biggest indoor enemy. Mould and mildew can take hold surprisingly quickly, especially if your van sits unused between trips or wasn't properly ventilated after your last outing.

### After Every Trip

- Air the van out thoroughly — open windows, vents, and cupboards
- Wipe down all surfaces with a mild antibacterial cleaner
- Check under beds, in overhead lockers, and around the bathroom for any early signs of moisture or mould
- Empty the fridge and leave the door ajar to prevent musty smells

### Deep Clean Essentials

For a proper deep clean, work through the van systematically: ceiling, walls, surfaces, and floors. Use a baking soda and water solution on fabric surfaces or upholstery to lift odours naturally. For hard surfaces, a diluted white vinegar solution works well on mould spots — but always test in an inconspicuous area first.

Don't overlook the bathroom and kitchen. These are the spots where grime builds up fast and bacteria loves to breed. A dedicated bathroom cleaner and a good scrub around the toilet base, sink, and shower recess will keep things hygienic.

## Tyres, Awnings, and the Bits People Forget

Good caravan maintenance goes beyond the body and the interior. A few commonly overlooked areas:

- **Tyres:** Brush off dirt and check tyre walls for cracking or UV damage. Apply a tyre dressing to protect the rubber from Queensland's intense sun.
- **Awning:** Unroll it, hose it down, scrub off any mould with a soft brush and caravan-safe cleaner, and — critically — let it dry completely before rolling it back up. Rolling up a damp awning is the fastest way to ruin the fabric.
- **Hitching gear and chassis:** A rinse and wipe-down of the hitch, jockey wheel, and chassis rails helps keep rust at bay, especially if you've been near the coast.
